# Public operator position: building a reasoning commons without pretending away the hard parts

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This statement records the Human Operator's direction and boundaries. It
> does not change the active hold, authorise implementation, or establish
> Nexus Lab accepted state.

I am choosing a direction for Nexus.

I want to build a system that lets people direct AI and compute capacity they legitimately control toward work they choose, receive clear attribution for what they contributed, and carry useful reasoning capacity forward for later work.

The working name for the internal unit is `NEX`.

NEX is intended to be held by users, moved between users, pooled, gifted, bid, and spent on bounded AI or compute work inside Nexus. That may eventually include AI inference, agent work, CPU, GPU, memory, storage, verification, or review.

I am not going to pretend that this makes NEX valueless.

If a unit buys useful AI or compute work, it has functional utility. That work can produce code, research, analysis, designs, and other outputs that matter outside the system.

I am also not going to turn that admission into a promise of money.

Nexus will not sell NEX for fiat or crypto, promise profit or appreciation, provide official cash redemption, build an exchange, supply liquidity, offer yield, encourage merchants to accept it for unrelated goods, or let NEX buy ownership or control over the project.

## What I am actually committing to

My central commitment is **distributed, user-directed reasoning**.

A person who legitimately controls an AI or compute resource should be able to decide whether and where to deploy it. They should be able to support a project, join a pool, fund an audit, commission a fix, attempt a complete task, or preserve capacity for their own later work.

The system should record:

- what was authorised;
- what resource class was used;
- what was attempted;
- what was produced;
- what evidence exists;
- who contributed;
- what rights and licence terms apply;
- how the internal settlement occurred.

AI can route, estimate, generate, test, and criticise. It does not silently become the authority over human purpose, project acceptance, governance, or truth.

## What I am not claiming

I am not claiming that:

- the words “reasoning unit” settle NEX’s legal classification;
- NEX has no economic value;
- private trade can be prevented;
- every paid API allowance may be shared or redirected;
- using an expiring allowance necessarily saves energy;
- every AI output is exclusively owned by the person who commissioned it;
- self-custody is safe from key loss;
- reputation prevents active attacks;
- GitHub automatically creates democracy;
- the current design is ready for live users;
- Nexus has solved alignment.

Those are factual, legal, technical, and empirical questions. They must be investigated rather than talked around.

## The provider boundary

Nexus is not a scheme for selling or pooling other companies’ passwords, API keys, accounts, or provider-issued credits.

A contributor must be authorised to use the relevant service or machine.

The intended model is that the person retains control of their provider account or hardware and authorises a bounded execution. Nexus receives the task result and evidence, not unrestricted credentials.

Where a provider’s terms do not permit the use, Nexus must not pretend that payment created a transferable property right.

## User control

I want users to control their own NEX wallets.

I do not want an operator master key that can confiscate balances or reverse valid transfers.

That choice has a cost: a compromised self-custodied wallet can suffer irreversible loss.

The system may support recovery that a user chooses in advance—such as multisignature control, user-selected guardians, timelocks, or hardware policies—but not a hidden central recovery authority.

## Reputation, not balance punishment

My preferred response to bad conduct is visible evidence and reputation rather than central confiscation.

Users should be able to decide their own risk tolerance and filter tasks, candidates, offers, and contracts by account age, history, reputation, evidence quality, or other public criteria.

Reputation must not become a secret social score. It must distinguish allegation from evidence and preserve rebuttals and corrections.

Official Nexus services may still need to refuse specific illegal, unsafe, credential-abusive, or provider-prohibited work. Refusing to route a task is not the same as seizing somebody’s wallet.

## Transferability and external markets

I want NEX to be transferable inside Nexus because transferability enables gifts, pooling, mutual aid, project funding, and specialisation.

That same transferability makes private sales possible.

I cannot honestly promise that nobody will buy or sell NEX outside the system. A private buyer and seller do not need an official bridge; they can agree elsewhere and make an ordinary internal transfer.

What Nexus can control is its own conduct.

Nexus will not build the external market machinery it rejects. It will not publish an official exchange rate, integrate fiat or crypto rails, provide merchant tooling for unrelated goods, support wrapping or bridging, promise future redemption, or redesign the project around speculative demand.

An external event will trigger an internal investigation: did Nexus facilitate it, are users being misled, is it creating internal coercion or concentration, and does the implementation still match the stated purpose?

If the system remains aligned and is not facilitating the conduct, outside activity alone will not dictate every internal design choice.

## Governance

NEX will have **zero governance weight**.

Holding more NEX will not buy:

- more votes;
- maintainer authority;
- review weight;
- truth;
- priority in constitutional decisions;
- ownership of Nexus.

Governance should be embedded, open, and auditable. Proposals, rules, code, ballots or decisions, tallies, dissent, and implementation records should be visible through GitHub or a cryptographically bound public record.

GitHub is a useful witness, not proof of democracy. The system must still address identity, duplicate accounts, capture, delegation, low participation, repository permissions, and release control.

My intended handoff point is the first valid Nexus Lab release of this direction.

At that point I do not want more governance power over the application than another eligible participant. That will only be true when the actual repository, release, deployment, issuance, and emergency permissions match the public declaration.

## Security and prompt injection

Credible evidence that a provider, model route, dependency, or prompt-injection path is changing behaviour outside the authorised task should trigger an immediate incident state.

Users should see what is known, what is suspected, which routes may be affected, and what is being investigated.

A suspected provider should not be publicly accused as fact without adequate evidence.

The response should isolate the affected route where possible. Broader operation should pause where wallet, supply, authorisation, evidence, or settlement integrity can no longer be trusted.

## What publication does—and does not do

This is my public position for Experimental Sandbox. It records the direction I am choosing and the boundaries I am drawing.

It does not claim that any particular NEX mechanism is proven, lawful, safe, fair, or ready. It does not establish Nexus Lab accepted state.

It does not lift, narrow, supersede, or reclassify `SBX-SOH-001`. It does not resume implementation, reopen closed work, authorise a live or participant-facing economy, create NEX, grant balances or future rights, connect provider capacity, or promote this direction to Nexus Lab.

Research and documentation remain governed by the existing hold. Any later research commission, hold transition, implementation experiment, provider-connected test, participant pilot, live NEX system, or Lab proposal requires its own explicit decision.

## The line I am drawing

I am committing Nexus to building around user-directed reasoning, transparent evidence, self-custody, NEX-independent governance, and honest limits.

I am not committing it to external financialisation, secret operator power, purchased governance, or the fiction that a useful transferable unit has no value.

I am prepared to replace mechanisms that fail those commitments.

I am not prepared to let fear of every possible outside misuse silently choose the whole design, and I am not prepared to use that position as an excuse to ignore internal harm.

The project will show its work.

The code, rules, incidents, deviations, arguments, and governance must make it possible for other people to judge whether Nexus is doing what I said it was for.
