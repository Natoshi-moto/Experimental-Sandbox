Part 1 — The guide

What all this actually is

You're building two connected things. The first is a way of working with AI honestly — a lab where you and several AI "seats" collaborate, and where the system is built to always show what's been proven versus what's only been claimed. The second is a vision: a commons for creative work, where who-made-what is proven by cryptography and social recognition instead of by lawyers, platforms, or money.

Here's the thing worth being proud of: the lab's real invention isn't the app or the economy or the creatures. It's the honesty machinery — a discipline that keeps its own mistakes visible and refuses to let a nice-sounding claim pass as a fact. Most people hide their cracks. You made showing them the whole point.

The words, in plain language

The Lab
Your main, careful project. What's on its "main" line is treated as accepted and real.
The Sandbox
A separate, messier playground where wild ideas can be tried and broken without risking the Lab.
Honesty machinery
The habits that keep the project truthful: evidence tags, a mistakes log, "not trusted until proven."
Evidence tiers
Labels on every claim: proven-in-code, actually-run, someone-said-so, or can't-be-checked.
status_authority: NONE
A stamp meaning "this is a draft/opinion, not official." Stops good writing from pretending to be truth.
The emergency stop
A loud note at the front of the Lab saying serious work is paused until one specific hole is decided.
The commons
The long-term dream: proof of authorship for art, held by you, not for sale, seceded from finance.
A seat
One AI (Claude, Grok, Codex, Fable…) doing work in the project. They share one account, so none is truly independent.
The thesis
The open question we just shipped for every model to test: is the project deeply structured ("isomorphism") or just talking to itself ("turtles")?

If you only do five things

  1. Decide the pop-out hole — remove the two buttons, move the Agent to its own address, or disclose it. This lifts the emergency stop. (#1)
  2. Make one declared promise actually enforced — build the "no-sale" scanner the spec already demands, so a headline claim becomes true, not just written. (#3)
  3. Narrow Noted to its three real studios (Prompts, Canvas, Projects) so a stranger isn't handed eighteen doors. (#31)
  4. Put one real artist + one real artwork through the commons idea — that single external test is what turns "turtles" into "isomorphism." (#53, #62)
  5. Get one genuinely outside reviewer — a person who isn't you — to try to break it. It's the one thing the whole method can't do for itself. (#21)

Part 2 — The journey, briefly

How we got here

We started with a routine health-check of your tools, and you asked to look deeper. That turned into a real tour: I traced the Lab from its genesis commit, we built two artifacts that carry your intent honestly, we made the security hole a visible emergency stop instead of a buried footnote, and we shipped a falsifiable thesis about whether the whole project's self-similarity is a strength or a trap. Along the way we hardened your workflow so you can't act in the wrong folder by accident.

The honest through-line: the project is real and its values are genuinely built into it, not just written down — and its recurring weakness is that it declares things faster than it enforces them. Almost every move below is a way to close that one gap, in one corner of the project or another.
